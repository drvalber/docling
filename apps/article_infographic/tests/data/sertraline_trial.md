# Sertraline versus placebo for late-life depression in primary care: a 12-week randomized controlled trial

Ana Beatriz Moreira, Carlos Eduardo Lima, Fernanda Ribeiro Santos, João Pedro Almeida

Journal of Affective Disorders. 2024. doi:10.1016/j.jad.2024.00001

Keywords: late-life depression; sertraline; primary care; randomized controlled trial

## Abstract

Background: Late-life depression is under-treated in primary care. We tested whether sertraline improves depressive symptoms in adults aged 65 years or older. Methods: Double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial in 14 primary care units. Results: Sertraline reduced HAM-D scores more than placebo (mean difference −4.1, 95% CI −5.6 to −2.6; p < 0.001). Response occurred in 58% versus 34% (OR = 2.7, 95% CI 1.6 to 4.5). Conclusions: Sertraline is effective and well tolerated for late-life depression in primary care.

## 1 Introduction

Depression affects up to 15% of older adults seen in primary care, yet fewer than half receive adequate treatment. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors are recommended first-line, but trial evidence in patients older than 65 years remains limited. We aimed to determine whether sertraline is superior to placebo over 12 weeks.

## 2 Methods

### 2.1 Design and participants

We conducted a double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial in 14 primary care units of a public health network. Participants were adults aged 65 years or older with a diagnosis of major depressive disorder and a baseline HAM-D score of at least 14. A total of n = 312 patients were randomized to sertraline (n = 156) or placebo (n = 156).

### 2.2 Intervention

Patients received sertraline 50 mg daily, titrated to 100 mg at week 4 if tolerated, or matching placebo. Follow-up visits occurred at weeks 4, 8 and 12.

### 2.3 Outcomes and statistical analysis

The primary outcome was the change in HAM-D score from baseline to week 12. Secondary outcomes were response (≥50% reduction in HAM-D), remission (HAM-D ≤ 7) and the GAD-7 anxiety score. Analyses followed the intention-to-treat principle using mixed models for repeated measures, with significance set at p < 0.05.

## 3 Results

### 3.1 Primary outcome

At week 12, HAM-D scores fell by 9.8 ± 5.2 points with sertraline and 5.7 ± 5.4 points with placebo (mean difference −4.1, 95% CI −5.6 to −2.6; p < 0.001).

### 3.2 Secondary outcomes

Response was achieved by 58% of the sertraline group and 34% of the placebo group (OR = 2.7, 95% CI 1.6 to 4.5; p < 0.001). Remission occurred in 41% versus 22% (OR = 2.5, 95% CI 1.5 to 4.2; p = 0.001). Anxiety improved modestly (GAD-7 mean difference −1.9, 95% CI −3.0 to −0.8; p = 0.001). Discontinuation due to adverse events was 6% with sertraline and 4% with placebo (p = 0.42).

## 4 Discussion

Sertraline produced a clinically meaningful reduction in depressive symptoms compared with placebo in older primary care patients. The effect size is consistent with meta-analyses in younger adults. In clinical practice, the results support sertraline as a first-line option for late-life depression when psychotherapy is unavailable. Limitations include the exclusion of patients with dementia, which limits generalizability, and the relatively short 12-week follow-up. The sample was also too small to detect rare adverse events.

## 5 Conclusion

Sertraline is superior to placebo for late-life depression treated in primary care and is well tolerated. Longer trials should evaluate relapse prevention and outcomes in patients with cognitive impairment.
